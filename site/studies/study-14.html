Study 14: what keeps Treasury repo outside central clearing? — result

Descriptive. Design: docs/design-study-14-haircuts-netting.md, committed before the data was fetched. It has one amendment, made after the first fetch and before any measure was computed: confidentiality suppression meant early weeks were incomplete. Every figure is read from result.json; the weekly series is in weekly.csv.

  • Data: the New York Fed's primary dealer statistics, weekly, January 2022 – October 2026, Treasury collateral excluding TIPS.
  • Haircut evidence: cited from the OFR, not recomputed.

In one line

Central clearing grew, but the uncleared market grew with it. What stays outside is a matched book of repo and reverse repo that dealers already net among themselves, mostly at zero haircut. Clearing would add margin to those trades without adding much netting, so they leave clearing again after each year-end.

The two windows:

  • The main version's early window is the first 13 complete weeks, scattered between October 2022 and May 2024, because sponsored term buckets were suppressed for confidentiality.
  • The sensitivity version treats suppressed values as zero and starts cleanly in January–March 2022. It gives an uncleared share of 60.0% → 57.2% and uncleared reverse repo of $884bn → $1,563bn, while sponsored reverse repo rose from $32bn to $507bn.

Both versions say "holding" (−6.3 and −2.8 points, against the design's 10-point bar). The growth of cleared repo has been mostly new business, not trades moving out of the uncleared market.

2. A large matched book sits outside clearing

Dealers' uncleared repo and reverse repo against specified Treasuries are of similar size. The smaller is 84% of the larger on average, 80% in the latest 13 weeks, against 71% in cleared specified and sponsored repo. That is the shape of netted packages: a dealer finances a client's long bond and lends it a different bond to short, so the two trades offset on the dealer's balance sheet without a clearinghouse.

  • Trend: the matched share has not shrunk since 2023–24 (81% → 80%). In the sensitivity version it fell from 96% in early 2022, when cleared volumes were small.
  • What it can't show: the data do not identify counterparties, so this bounds netted packages from above; it cannot count them.
  • Corroboration: the OFR, using trade-level data, estimates that about 68% of hedge funds' reverse repo is netted this way.

The uncleared share dips at every year-end, when banks' G-SIB capital surcharge is measured and netting through the clearinghouse pays. It rebounds within weeks. This matches the OFR's account of the 2026 plateau. Clearing is used when it lowers dealers' capital charges, and abandoned when it doesn't.

The OFR's 2026 analysis adds that hedge funds' reverse repos often carry negative haircuts, which FICC's margining struggles to accommodate.

Answering the questions:

  • Is it the haircuts? In large part. The trades that stay outside clearing are the ones where clearing costs the most and adds the least: matched packages at zero or negative haircuts, which dealers already net.
  • Does it remove the risk? Zero haircuts on offsetting trades are low-risk for the dealer. But if a client fails in a fast market, the dealer holds no cushion on either leg.
  • Avoiding reporting: no sign of it. These trades are reported to the OFR by the dealers.
  • "Trust-me" (hold-in-custody) repo: no sign of it. The pattern is netting, not custody.

What it means for the June 2027 deadline: the trades with the least incentive to move are the netted, zero-haircut packages, and the trades between affiliates whose exemption the industry is contesting. The year-end pattern shows these trades can be cleared when the economics say so. The question for the deadline is the margin they will carry once they must.

Limits

  • Primary dealers only. Counterparties are not identified.
  • Matched-book ratio: it bounds netted packages from above; it cannot count them.
  • Confidentiality suppression: it affects the early weeks; both versions are reported.
  • Haircuts: the levels are the OFR's (January–May 2025), not measured here.
