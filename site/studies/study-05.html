Study 05: does repo stress spread outward, beyond calendar days? (H3) — result

Design: docs/design-study-05-spillback.md, committed before the run. Every figure is read from result.json. The daily swap series built here is committed as swap_rates_daily.csv. DTCC keeps only a rolling two-year window, so these numbers will outlive the files they came from.

Verdict: H3 not supported

Neither part meets its rule.

Part A: repo shocks the calendar does not explain

Shocks: days with SOFR − IORB up at least 5 bp that are not calendar, FOMC or IORB-change days.

  • 2018–19: seven days, including 2019-09-12 and 2019-09-24 around the September 2019 spike.
  • 2025–26: seven days: 2025-04-08, 2025-06-25, 2025-07-25, 2025-10-21, 2025-12-26, 2026-05-26, 2026-07-13.
  • The floor regime: none.

Outcome: the change over the shock day and the next.

After the repo shocks of 2025–26, broad market stress did not rise; if anything it eased slightly.

Secondary outcomes: 4 of 32 intervals exclude zero, against about 1.6 by chance.

  • 2018–19: larger 10-year moves (+2.8 bp in size [+0.1, +5.9]) and larger bill moves (+3.4 bp [+0.4, +7.0]).
  • 2025–26: Baa − 10y rose +1.3 bp [+0.4, +2.2] over the two days, with smaller-than-usual moves in size (−1.2 bp [−2.0, −0.4]). Credit widened by a little, consistently.

That last one is the only hint of spillback in the current regime. It is one of 32 comparisons on 7 days, so it is a lead to watch, not a finding.

Part B: swap spreads, November 2024 – September 2026

Data: daily 2-year and 10-year SOFR swap rates, the notional-weighted median of 98,052 qualifying trades over 516 days of DTCC's public reports. The swap spread is the swap rate minus the Treasury yield.

  • The 10-year spread averaged −46 bp, ranging from −70 to −33.
  • April 2025 checks out: it widened from −45 bp (1 Apr) to −61 bp (10 Apr), matching the swap-spread collapse reported at the time, then recovered.

The hypothesis predicted a negative slope and a negative correlation: repo pressure cheapening Treasuries against swaps. The slope's sign is right, but it is about zero, and month-end pressure is unrelated to swap spreads.

What this means for the question

Read together with study 01 (calendar days), H3 now has its broadest public-data test, and the answer is the same: repo pressure in 2025–26 has not spread to wider markets at daily frequency. That holds for broad stress, yields, credit, equities, the dollar, and even swap spreads, the price most exposed to dealers' balance sheets.

The single hint is a 1.3 bp rise in Baa spreads after the seven largest non-calendar repo shocks.

April 2025 shows the direction again. Swap spreads widened by 16 bp in a week while repo moved 7 bp: the bond market's stress reached swaps, and repo followed modestly. Repo did not lead.

Limits

  • Seven shocks per regime: a null is weak.
  • Part B has no comparison regime. DTCC's window starts in November 2024.
  • Prints: swap rates come from trades, not quotes. The 2-year series has two outlying prints: −96 bp on 2026-10-21, outside the panel's dates, and −55 bp on 2026-08-03. Neither affects the 10-year main test.
