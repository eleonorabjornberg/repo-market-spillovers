Study 23: the dealer markup as a daily series — result

Design: docs/design-study-23-dealer-markup.md, committed before this study's new data was fetched. Every figure is read from result.json.

The markup is SOFR's 99th percentile minus tri-party's (TGCR's) 99th percentile, in bp. It is the premium the dearest trades in cleared bilateral repo pay over the dearest in tri-party. The series runs 2 April 2018 – 23 October 2026 (2,137 days).

In one line

The markup is a balance-sheet price. It is about zero when reserves are abundant, about 7 bp on ordinary days when they are scarce, and it jumps at every quarter-end, to about 31 bp at year-ends, when banks' balance sheets are measured. The Fed's full allotment did not touch it, and the growth of sponsored clearing has not shrunk it.

The premium for cleared bilateral repo jumps when dealers' balance sheets are measured. Quarter-ends are reporting dates for many banks, and year-end is when the G-SIB surcharge is set. This is the pattern a price for balance sheet would show.

Q2, full allotment: no change (reported)

On non-calendar days, the markup averaged 7.2 bp in 2025 before full allotment and 6.8 bp after: −0.4 bp [−0.8, +0.05]. Full allotment caps what dealers pay for cash, not what they charge clients, as study 19 read it.

Q3, sponsored clearing: no detectable narrowing (verdict)

Across 79 months since March 2020, the monthly markup on non-calendar days and the sponsored share of cleared repo are positively correlated, at +0.63 [+0.09, +0.91]. That is not because clearing raises the markup. Both trended with the return of scarce reserves.

Limits

  • Percentiles are volume-weighted. Some of the quarter-end jump may be the volume mix shifting between segments.
  • Counterparties are not observed. "Markup to clients" remains a reading.
  • Q3 compares trends. The table, not the correlation, is the evidence.
