Study 12: looking for better warning indicators — result

Design: docs/design-study-12-indicators.md, committed before any candidate was scored, with one amendment before scoring: the excess bond premium (L2) was dropped from the composite and scored beside it once Eleonora supplied the file. Every figure is read from result.json. 22 stress events, 1990–2025. Each month, each indicator is scored by its percentile in its own history up to that month, using only what was public then.

Verdict: "promising" by the rule, but no reliable early warning was found

Main test (test period 2013–2025, 6 months ahead): the composite of four long-history candidates against the yield curve gives −0.02 [−0.23, +0.16]. No better than the curve.

Reading it honestly

  • The two "promising" candidates are where to look, not answers.
    • Low long-run VIX beat chance on 2013–2025 at 12 months, but was worse than a coin flip on 1993–2012. A signal that flips between periods is not reliable; it may reflect that the 2013–2025 episodes mostly arrived after long calm spells.
    • Leverage of the largest hedge funds did best of all (0.74), but Form PF only starts in 2013, so it has no development period to check against. It is the most interesting candidate, and the least tested.
  • The rest found nothing. The credit gap, the excess bond premium, the stocks–bonds correlation and the composite were coin flips. The same holds for the credit-spread benchmark: tight spreads did not precede trouble in this sample.
  • The reason is in the events. Most of these 22 episodes were sudden shocks: 9/11, the flash crashes, COVID, the tariff announcement. No slow-moving balance-sheet indicator could time them. The literature's successes are with banking crises over years, not market stress over months.

What to do with it: as the design said, the two promising candidates go into the monthly watch as readings, to be judged on events that have not happened yet. Their latest readings (percentile in their own history):

  • Top-10 hedge-fund leverage: 72% (as of June 2026's report, public by about December 2026).
  • VIX 12-month average: 50% (September 2026), middling.

Limits

  • Overlapping events and labels: 22 events with 6- and 12-month labels overlap, and the yearly block bootstrap only partly allows for that.
  • Form PF's publication lag is assumed to be two quarters.
  • The credit gap is a simplified trailing mean, not the BIS's one-sided filter.
