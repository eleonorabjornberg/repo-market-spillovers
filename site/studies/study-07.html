Part 1: is the growth of cleared repo a migration ahead of the mandate? Supported

This tests Eleonora's point that traders have been preparing for the SEC's June 2027 repo-clearing deadline for years. Each series gets a segmented trend, with breaks fixed in advance at the SEC's adoption of the rule (Dec 2023) and its extension of the deadlines (Feb 2025). Slopes are in percentage points per year; intervals come from a moving-block bootstrap.

Both decisive series meet the rule, so the verdict is supported. FICC's share of money-fund repo had been flat for four years (15.7% in June 2019, 16.2% in November 2023). It then rose to 35.9% in the 14 months after the rule was adopted. When the deadline was pushed back, the climb slowed to a tenth of that pace, and the share was 39.6% in September 2026. Both breaks are sharp, and both fall on the regulatory dates.

Post hoc check, the Fed's RRP. Money funds pulled most of their cash out of the Fed's reverse repo facility in 2023–24, the same window, which lifts FICC's share of their total repo mechanically. Excluding the Fed, FICC's share of their private repo shows the same shape:

Cash leaving the RRP could still have chosen sponsored repo partly for other reasons, so this narrows the confounder but does not remove it.

What this changes: study 03 read FICC's growing share as concentration, a possible warning sign. Much of it is the market doing what the regulator required, ahead of time, which nets positions and eases dealers' balance sheets. The risk it creates is different: more of the system's cash now depends on one central counterparty, FICC, working on the worst day.

Erratum (26 October 2026, study 13)

The test above passed by its pre-registered rule, but its description overstates the timing. A first break in mid- to late 2022, around the rule's proposal (14 September 2022), fits all three series better than December 2023 (study 13, C6, post hoc). "Flat for four years (15.7% in June 2019, 16.2% in November 2023)" compared two endpoints: the share fell to 2–4% in 2022, when money funds' cash sat at the Fed's reverse repo facility, and rose from late 2022. The slowdown after the February 2025 extension holds under every start date; saturation is an alternative explanation not ruled out.
