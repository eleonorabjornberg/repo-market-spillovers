Study 22: what should reserves be measured against? — result

Design: docs/design-study-22-which-denominator.md, committed before this study's new data was fetched. Six denominators were fixed in advance. Every figure is read from result.json. Months April 2018 – October 2026, with March–December 2020 left out (93 months). Comparable months, those equally tight on ordinary days, are 15 in 2018–19 and 20 in 2025–26.

In one line

It is the debt, however it is counted. All three debt-based measures put the two scarce periods at the same level: debt held by the public, total public debt, and reserves plus the Treasury's cash over the debt. None of the three others does: GDP, bank assets, and the size of the repo market. Over time all six track repo tightness about equally well, so the fit cannot choose between them. The match can.

Verdict (pre-set): debt held by the public is not beaten. Total public debt fits as well within the intervals, and the three debt measures are within a few points of each other on the match.

What it means

  • Every way of counting the government's debt gives the same threshold; the economy and the banking system do not. That is the stronger version of study 20's finding, and less likely to be luck: three related measures agree, and three unrelated ones fail together.
  • Reserves now fall well short against the repo market's own size, at 61% of 2018–19's ratio. Repo volume has grown faster than the debt, largely because trading moved into the measured, cleared segments. That is why the design warned it was not a clean measure of need.
  • Adding the Treasury's cash account changes nothing, at 103%. The threshold is not an artefact of tax-date swings in that account.
  • Correlations over time do not discriminate, because everything trended together over these years. The comparison of two equally tight periods does.

Limits

  • Six tries. Three matched, all from one family, which is reassuring rather than suspicious. Each was still tried on data already seen in part.
  • Two scarce periods remain the whole basis of the match. Study 21 tests the gauge on months to come.
  • Description, not cause. Why reserves must keep pace with the debt is a reading (Treasury settlement, financing and the Treasury's cash flows all scale with it), not something this study measures.
