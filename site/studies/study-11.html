The answer, as far as public data goes

The repo still funds Treasuries, and the Treasuries are now hedged less with futures and more with rate derivatives. The growth sits mainly with multi-strategy funds, not macro funds.

Form PF's borrowing by strategy covers all borrowing, not repo alone. Equity funds borrow mostly through prime brokers, so their growth is unlikely to be repo. Multi-strategy funds run relative-value "pods", including Treasury trades.

TIC (decides nothing): the Cayman Islands' Treasury holdings, the usual proxy for hedge funds, rose from $411bn to $463bn (+12.8%) between August 2025 and August 2026. Over the same months leveraged funds' futures short fell 24.2%. Treasury's current table covers only those 13 months.

Reading it together

  • The repo still funds Treasuries. Repo and long Treasury holdings grew in step (E4: no), so the gap is not repo quietly funding something else.
  • The hedge changed. Long Treasuries rose about $390bn while the futures short fell about $170bn and rate-derivative exposure rose about $4.4tn gross. The simplest fit is long Treasuries hedged with swaps instead of futures: the swap-spread trade, or a mix of hedges. DTCC's package flag does not confirm it. It is a blunt proxy: 70–77% of SOFR swap notional is packaged in every month, mostly for reasons other than swap spreads, so a shift in one use may not show.
  • Still arbitrage, mostly in multi-strategy platforms. Macro funds' borrowing barely grew (+$16bn). The growth sits with multi-strategy and relative-value funds, whose Treasury positions are typically hedged relative-value trades, not outright bets on rates.

What it means for risk: a hedged Treasury position financed in repo is still leveraged, and still has to be unwound if funding or margins tighten. A swap hedge brings a different risk from a futures hedge: the swap-spread widening of April 2025 (−61 bp, study 05) was exactly the move that hurts it. So the exposure Eleonora pointed to is real. It is just carried in swaps more than futures now, which public data sees less clearly.

Limits

  • Aggregates: what kind of trade, not who.
  • Form PF is quarterly and about two quarters behind.
  • Proxies: DTCC packages are a weak proxy for swap-spread trades, and borrowing by strategy is not repo alone.
  • TIC covers 13 months.
  • Not public yet: the OFR's uncleared bilateral repo collection.
