All studies

Every study in the project, each designed and committed before its data was examined. Start with the findings for the overall picture.

Event study 01

No, at daily closes in public data. Weak signs only in bills and overnight CP.

Study 02: then and now

Not on the measures that flashed then. New this time: record hedge-fund repo, record public debt, very tight credit spreads, with no history to score them against.

Study 03: map of the chain (H1)

Not supported as specified. Hedge-fund repo is at a record relative to the market and money funds' cash runs increasingly through FICC, but the basis-trade proxy is below 2019 and shrinking, and lending is spread across more counterparty types.

Study 04: do Treasury-market shocks spread into repo? (H2)

Nominally "supported for 2025–26 only" (+1.1 bp next day), but weak: a placebo is 84% as large, no replication, no dose–response. April 2025 moved repo 7 bp.

Study 05: does repo stress spread outward, beyond calendar days? (H3)

Not supported. Broad stress eased slightly after 2025–26 repo shocks; swap-spread pass-through about zero. Only hint: Baa +1.3 bp.

Study 06: would the chain have warned? (H4)

Cannot tell. Best chain indicator (basis-trade proxy) AUROC 0.63 against the yield curve's 0.58; difference +0.05 [−0.14, +0.24]. Nothing warned reliably.

Study 07: the clearing migration, and the watch

Supported: FICC's share of money-fund repo was flat for four years, then rose 21 points a year after the rule's adoption (Dec 2023) and slowed after the extension (Feb 2025). The watch's first reading (Sep 2026): 1 of 8 flags on (complacency).

Study 09: Treasuries in a panic, and household debt

Not supported: the weaker haven since 2020 tracks inflation, not debt; repo moves less after panic sell-offs; household debt is down from 98% to 66% of GDP.

Study 10: three prospective tests

Tests set on 26 October 2026, with the code that scores them, before their data exists: the clearing deadlines, the credit lead, and the chain under the next stress. No result yet, by design; this page shows where each stands.

Study 11: what is hedge-fund repo funding now that the basis trade has shrunk?

Still Treasuries (repo +21%, long Treasuries +20%), hedged less with futures (−16%) and more with rate derivatives (+47%); growth with multi-strategy and relative-value funds, not macro.

Study 12: looking for better warning indicators

No reliable one. Composite = yield curve (−0.02). Two "promising": largest hedge funds' leverage (0.74, but only from 2013) and low long-run VIX (0.78 at 12m, but below chance before 2013). Both now tracked in the watch.

Study 13: pre-publication checks

One withdrawn (seasonal dip in hedge-fund clearing); basis-trade decline 17% by risk, not 30%; the swap shift is already published by the Fed; clearing's rise began with the 2022 proposal (erratum to study 07).

Study 14: what keeps Treasury repo outside central clearing?

The uncleared market grew with clearing (dealers' uncleared reverse repo $0.9tn → $1.6tn; share 60% → 57%). Dealers' uncleared repo and reverse repo are 80–84% matched (netted packages), mostly at zero haircut per the OFR; trades enter clearing at each year-end and leave again.

Study 15: corporate bonds in repo, and in a stock-market panic

Corporate collateral is a small corner of repo ($167bn of dealers' repo against $3.4tn on Treasuries). When Treasuries fail as a haven in a panic, corporate yields rise too on 89% of days: no offset. The cushion depends on Treasuries rallying. In 2008, dealers' financing against corporate securities fell 42% while Treasury financing fell 17%: the run hit private collateral.

Study 16: data-centre debt, and whether it reaches the swap market

Debt of Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle and CoreWeave up $95bn in a year to $334bn. Long-dated swap trading is no heavier around their bond deals (+6% [−16%, +30%]): the channel is not visible in public data.

Study 17: why 2025–26 month-ends are milder than 2018–19's, and what drove the slow creep

Ordinary month-ends are not milder; the extremes are (year-end 2018 +62 bp; since the standing repo facility the worst turn is 22 bp above its rate): a leaky ceiling. The creep goes mostly with the reverse repo facility draining, then reserves.

Study 19: who pays above the facility's line, and did they use the facility?

The expensive trades sit in cleared bilateral repo, not tri-party, and on most such days the facility is idle: since April 2026 they are a 1–5 bp dealer markup over a ceiling that caps dealers' own funding.
